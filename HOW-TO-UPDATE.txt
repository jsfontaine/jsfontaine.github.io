HOW TO UPDATE YOUR WEBSITE
==========================

You do not need any special software. You need a plain text editor
(TextEdit on a Mac, Notepad on Windows, or the editor built into GoDaddy's
File Manager) and the ability to upload a file.

There is no compiling, no building, no installing. What you edit is exactly
what visitors see.


WHAT EACH FILE IS
-----------------
  index.html        the About page (your photo and bio)
  research.html     working papers, journal articles, book chapters, archived
  policy.html       the Bank of Canada notes and discussion papers
  contact.html      the contact page
  style.css         all the styling for all three pages
  papers/           your PDFs, one per paper, plus CV.pdf
  data/             the two published datasets
  media/photo.jpg   your photo
  fonts/            the two typefaces, served from your own server


TO ADD A NEW PAPER
------------------
1. Put the PDF in the papers/ folder. Give it a simple lowercase name with
   hyphens instead of spaces, for example:  new-paper-title.pdf

2. Open research.html in a text editor.

3. Find the section it belongs in — search for the words
   "WORKING PAPERS" or "JOURNAL PUBLICATIONS". (Bank of Canada notes
   live in policy.html instead.)

4. Find an existing paper that looks like the one you're adding. It is a
   block that starts with  <li class="paper">  and ends with  </li>
   Select that whole block, copy it, and paste it directly above or below.

5. In your pasted copy, change the words between the tags:
     - between <span class="title"> and </span>  ... the paper title
     - after "with"                              ... the coauthors
     - between <span class="venue"> and </span>  ... the journal name
     - the year
     - the file name after  href="/papers/       ... your new PDF

6. Save the file and upload it to the server, replacing the old one.

That's the whole job. If you paste the block and change nothing else, the
new entry will look exactly like every other one.


TO CHANGE YOUR BIO
------------------
Open index.html and edit the text between the <p> and </p> tags. Ignore
everything else in the file.


A FEW RULES THAT WILL SAVE YOU TROUBLE
--------------------------------------
* Every <tag> has a matching </tag>. Keep them in pairs. If something looks
  broken after an edit, you have almost certainly deleted half of a pair.

* Don't rename files unless you also change every place they're linked.

* & must be written as &amp; inside the page — that's why the S&P entry
  reads "S&amp;P". Similarly, an en-dash is &ndash;.

* Always keep a copy of the previous version before you upload a new one.
  Then any mistake is thirty seconds from being undone.

* To check your work before uploading: double-click the .html file on your
  own computer. It opens in your browser and looks exactly as it will on the
  site. The PDF links will work too.


IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
-----------------------
Upload your previous copy of the file. The site returns to how it was.
Nothing else can break, because nothing else is moving.

THE HIDDEN PHOTO
----------------
Clicking the round portrait on the About page swaps in a second picture;
clicking again swaps back. It uses no JavaScript — just a hidden checkbox and
two images, and the stylesheet shows whichever one matches.

To change the second picture: put your new image in the media/ folder and,
in index.html, change the filename on the line marked "photo-b". Nothing else
needs touching. Resize it to roughly 1200 pixels wide first so the page stays
quick to load.

To remove it entirely: in index.html, delete the <input> and <label> block and
put back a plain line reading
    <img src="media/photo.jpg" alt="Jean-Sebastien Fontaine">

A SHORTER COMPANION PIECE
-------------------------
If a paper also has a plain-language summary or blog post, it goes underneath
the paper rather than as its own entry. Copy this line into the paper's block,
just before the closing </li>, and change the address and the wording:

    <span class="actions"><a href="THE-WEB-ADDRESS" rel="noopener">Plain-language summary</a></span>

See "Will Asset Managers Dash for Cash?" in policy.html for a live example.
